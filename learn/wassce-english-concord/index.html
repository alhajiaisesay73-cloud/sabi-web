Concord in WASSCE English: rules and practice questions

Concord means agreement: the verb must agree with its subject. One person or thing takes a singular verb (is, has, goes). Two or more take a plural verb (are, have, go). Below are the rules WASSCE tests most and 10 practice questions with answers.

How to answer it

Each, every, either, neither: singular
These words point at one thing at a time, so the verb is singular.Each of the girls HAS a phone.
Neither...nor, either...or: the nearer noun wins
The verb agrees with the noun closest to it.Neither the driver nor the passengers WERE hurt.
Words in the middle do not count
Phrases like "together with", "as well as" and "of the boys" do not change the subject.The principal, as well as the teachers, IS at the meeting. One of my friends LIVES in Kenema.
The number of / a number of
"The number of" is one number, so it is singular. "A number of" means many, so it is plural.The number of cars in Freetown IS rising. A number of pupils ARE absent today.
Tricky nouns
News, Mathematics and furniture take a singular verb. Trousers, scissors and police take a plural verb.The news on the radio WAS sad. These scissors ARE sharp.

Answer: C. is

Answer: B. does

Questions students ask

What is concord in English?

Concord is agreement between parts of a sentence, mostly between the subject and the verb. A singular subject takes a singular verb, and a plural subject takes a plural verb.

Is it "neither of them is" or "neither of them are"?

In WASSCE English it is "neither of them is". "Neither of" means not one of the two, so it takes a singular verb.

Why does concord matter in WASSCE?

WAEC's Chief Examiner's report for 2023 lists wrong concord among the common mistakes candidates make. Concord also comes up in Paper 1 structure questions.

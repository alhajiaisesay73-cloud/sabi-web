Japan and WFP sign US$1.25 million grant for school meals in Pujehun

Published 6 November 2026, updated 9 November 2026 by the Sabi team.

Japan, the World Food Programme (WFP) and the Government of Sierra Leone have signed a JPY 200 million (about US$1.25 million) grant to give school meals to nearly 30,000 children in Pujehun District, as reported on 6 November 2026. The money is Japan's 2026 Kennedy Round Food Aid Grant. It covers 135 government-assisted pre-primary and primary schools in six chiefdoms, for two years.

Who will get the school meals?

Nearly 30,000 children in 135 government-assisted pre-primary and primary schools. The schools are in six chiefdoms of Pujehun District. The reports we read do not name the chiefdoms or the schools.

How long will the grant last?

Two years. The reports do not say the exact month the meals start.

What will the money pay for?

WFP will buy and deliver the food for the school meals. It will also train people at school level to run the programme. Part of the rice will be bought from local smallholder farmers, through the Home-Grown School Feeding approach. This gives farmers a steady buyer for their rice.

Where was the grant signed?

At the New Brookfields Hotel in Freetown. People from the Government of Japan, the Government of Sierra Leone, WFP, JICA and the National School Feeding Secretariat were there. The Deputy Minister of Basic and Senior Secondary Education, Emily Kadiatu Gogra, welcomed the grant. She said it helps the government keep school feeding going while it builds its own capacity.

Has Japan done this before?

Yes. The Japanese Ambassador, Hiroshi Yoshimoto, said earlier Kennedy Round grants already helped WFP give school meals to thousands of children in Pujehun and Kambia districts.
